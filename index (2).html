The three languages I live and work in

I grew up with Hindi as the language of home, stories and song, and with Sanskrit (संस्कृतम्) beneath it, in shlokas, prayers and the roots of the words we use every day.

Somewhere along the way I understood that a language is much more than grammar. It carries a way of looking at the world, a way of belonging to people, and a way of being heard. When a child learns Hindi, they are also learning that their voice has a place.

Our tradition calls sound sacred: नाद ब्रह्म. I trained as a Hindustani classical vocalist, and music taught me what the classroom later confirmed. Learning begins with listening. A child must first feel safe enough to be heard before they will risk speaking.

For more than eight years I have taught Hindi in Panipat, Gurgaon and now Bengaluru, mostly to children for whom it is an additional language. At Kunskapsskolan I worked with colleagues in Sweden on the KED framework, where every student sets their own goals and walks with a coach. At Anandi I teach Hindi from Grade 1 to Grade 8, lead Anandi Srijan for MYP, and have recently been selected to teach in the IB Diploma Programme.

My favourite moment in any class is quiet and small. A child who once struggled to write a single letter stands up and speaks in Hindi without being asked. That moment is why I teach.

मेरी सोच · What I believe

Four beliefs I carry into every classroom

भाषा सबकी है

Language belongs to everyone

No child stands outside a language. Some are only at its doorstep. I teach in phases so that the beginner and the fluent speaker in the same room both cross a threshold each term, and I keep evidence of it, because hope alone is not enough.

पहले सुनो

Listening comes before speaking

Music taught me that every voice begins in silence. I make room for that silence. A classroom where children feel heard is one where they will dare to speak, sing, act and make mistakes out loud.

करके सीखो

We become what we practise

A word memorised for a test fades. A word used to run a campaign, perform a play or teach a younger child stays. My students use Hindi to do real things, and that is how it becomes theirs.

स्वयं को जानो

Reflection is how we meet ourselves

At the end of every unit, students write what they did not know before, what they know now, what gave them joy and where they will go next. In naming their own growth, they learn to own it.

कक्षा · In my classroom

One language, eight years of a child's life

I am the only Hindi teacher at Anandi, so I meet every learner from Grade 1 to Grade 8.

That is a rare gift. I can design one connected journey through the language and watch a child grow along it, year after year: the six-year-old tracing her first अक्षर becomes the thirteen-year-old writing a speech for her campaign.

चरण

A curriculum built in phases

I designed a phase-based Hindi curriculum for Grades 1–8, so each learner is taught at the stage they have reached, not the one their age assumes. Children who start with little or no Hindi get a clear path forward. This year I track every learner against the phase descriptors after each unit.

सहारा

A scaffold for every learner

Tiered tasks, scaffolds, speaking supports and stretch options mean that every child in the room has work that fits them. I am gathering these strategies into a toolkit so colleagues can use them too.

कला

Learning through art and action

Students learn through theatre, storytelling and music, and through projects with a real audience, such as the healthy-living campaign Svasth Jeevan Abhiyaan and the hands-on kits they made in Unit 1.

सृजन

Anandi Srijan (MYP)

Srijan means creation. I lead Anandi Srijan for MYP, guiding students as they build portfolios that show who they are becoming. This year I am setting clear guidelines and structured feedback for every student.

लक्ष्य · Professional goals 2026–27

From my classroom to the whole school

I ask my students to name where they are and where they will go next. These goals are my own answer to the same question.

This year I want to work more systematically: planning around learner phases, gathering evidence of progress, inviting others to check my judgements, and sharing what works so it reaches more classrooms than mine.

१

Phase-based Hindi curriculum in practice and learner progress

A curriculum on paper is a promise. This goal asks whether it keeps that promise to every child.

By the end of Unit 5, I will implement the phase-based Hindi curriculum across Grades 1–8 and evaluate its impact on learner progress through tracking data, work samples and student feedback, with Grades 6 and 7 as an in-depth sample. I will document refinements after each unit and present a short summary of findings and recommendations.
